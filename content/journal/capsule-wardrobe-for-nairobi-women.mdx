A Nairobi capsule wardrobe is a small set of pieces that combine into many outfits. Start with twenty: tailoring, tops, bottoms, one or two dresses, a light layer, shoes and accessories in a coherent colour palette. If the pieces pair with each other, twenty is enough for forty-plus outfits — and a calmer 7am.

## What a capsule actually is

A capsule is not a uniform, and it is not a rule that you may only own twenty things. It is a system: a deliberately chosen set of pieces that work together, so getting dressed becomes combination rather than invention.

The point is not minimalism for its own sake. The point is that every piece has a job and most pieces talk to each other. A capsule for Nairobi also has to handle a specific reality: warm days, cool mornings and evenings, unpredictable rain, and a social calendar that moves from office to church to wedding to lunch.

## Choose a palette first

Before you buy or edit anything, choose two neutrals and two accents. That is the whole palette.

- **Neutrals:** cream, oatmeal, navy, black, cocoa, grey, white.
- **Accents:** cobalt, tangerine, vermilion, sun yellow, burgundy, olive.

Pick neutrals that suit your skin and your life, then add accents you actually enjoy wearing. A palette of cream, navy, cobalt and tangerine will produce more coherent outfits than a wardrobe of beautiful pieces in twelve unrelated colours.

If you already own a lot of one colour, build around it rather than starting again. A capsule is supposed to use what you have.

## The 20-piece list

This is a starting framework, not a shopping list to copy blindly. Adjust the ratios to your actual week.

### Tailoring & outer layers (4)

1. A navy or black blazer that fits your shoulders.
2. A wide-leg tailored trouser in a neutral.
3. A belted suit jacket or soft structured jacket in a second neutral.
4. A light trench, overshirt or cardigan for cool mornings and air-conditioned rooms.

### Tops (5)

5. A white or cream shirt with a relaxed fit.
6. A ruched or fitted shirt in an accent colour.
7. A fine knit or long-sleeve top in a neutral.
8. A sleeveless or short-sleeve top that works under tailoring.
9. A printed or textured top for personality.

### Bottoms (3)

10. Dark straight-leg jeans.
11. A pleated midi skirt in a neutral or print.
12. A tailored short or fluid wide-leg trouser in a lighter neutral.

### Dresses (2)

13. A midi dress in a solid colour that works for church, lunch or a wedding guest.
14. A dress in a print or stronger colour for celebrations.

### Shoes (3)

15. A neutral block-heel or low heel you can walk in.
16. Flat sandals or elegant slides for daytime.
17. A clean sneaker or comfortable flat for errands and travel.

### Accessories (3)

18. A small structured bag for evening and events.
19. A practical everyday bag that fits your phone, wallet and a notebook.
20. One jewellery story: gold hoops, a chain, a watch — whatever you reach for daily.

That is twenty. It is not a lot, which is exactly why the pieces have to work.

## The outfit maths

Twenty pieces do not automatically make forty outfits. Combinations do. The trick is to build in formulas:

- **Tailoring + top + shoe.** Four tailoring pieces and five tops already give twenty combinations before you add a skirt or dress.
- **Dress + layer + shoe + bag.** Two dresses with two layers and three shoes give twelve looks.
- **Skirt + top + jacket.** A pleated midi with three tops and two jackets gives six more.

The real number depends on how well the colours and proportions agree. If everything is in the same palette and the silhouettes are complementary, you will get there quickly. If each piece is a statement in its own right, you will have twenty outfits of one piece each.

## Make it work for Nairobi

A Nairobi capsule needs layers more than it needs heavy coats. Keep a light trench, overshirt or cardigan by the door. Choose fabrics that breathe — cotton, linen blends, crepe, silk blends — and avoid pieces that only work in air conditioning.

Rain is real. A block heel or a shoe you can wipe clean will earn its place more than a suede stiletto. A crossbody bag that keeps your phone dry is not a compromise; it is good design.

And your calendar matters. If most of your week is office and church, weight the capsule toward tailoring and midi dresses. If you attend weddings and events often, add a second occasion dress and a stronger shoe. If you work from home, spend the budget on the pieces you actually put on for the school run, lunch and the days you leave the house.

## What not to include

- **Pieces that only work with one other item.** They are not capsule pieces; they are guests.
- **Shoes you cannot walk in.** A capsule that hurts is not a capsule.
- **A colour you love on the hanger but never wear.** Be honest about the difference between admiration and use.
- **Duplicates in slightly different shades.** Two nearly identical black tops are one top and one missed opportunity.
- **Clothes for a future body or a future life.** Build for now; adjust later.

## How to start without buying anything

Pull out the pieces you already own that fit the list. Most women already have ten or twelve of the twenty. Put them together, photograph the combinations and live with them for a week.

The gaps will become obvious. A neutral shoe, a shirt that fits properly, a bag that works for evening — buy those slowly, one at a time, and only when they unlock several outfits. That is how a capsule stays useful instead of becoming another pile of good intentions.
